# Geslaagd Momentum — composition and content

## The job of the interface

Geslaagd helps a learner move from uncertainty to one credible next step. Every
surface should make that route feel shorter: state what is known, show why it is
trustworthy, and make the next action obvious.

## Surface and colour

- **Dark ink is the default learning environment.** Use it for the dashboard,
  coach, study spaces, source review, and focused reading. Warm ivory text keeps
  long sessions comfortable.
- **Light paper is a companion mode, not a second identity.** Use it where
  scanning, printing, account forms, or administrative density benefit from more
  daylight. Preserve the same hierarchy and emerald action.
- **Aurora is a local signal.** Emerald marks momentum and verified progress;
  blue supports evidence and navigation; violet marks selection and discovery.
  Keep gradients to bounded, high-energy moments such as a marketing hero or
  coach invitation. Never put them behind paragraphs, tables, or every card.
- **Glass is restrained.** A translucent panel may sit over an aurora moment,
  but it still needs a readable surface, defined border, and solid fallback.
  General content cards are opaque forest or paper surfaces.
- **Depth comes from layers, not shadows.** Use a border first. Add soft,
  low-contrast elevation only when a prompt, dialog, or active card rises above
  its parent.

## Hierarchy and composition

1. Start every state with a short outcome-led title.
2. Pair it with a single primary action. Secondary actions are quiet and nearby.
3. Keep explanatory copy short; let space separate decisions.
4. Put source and progress signals next to the claim or task they describe.
5. On small screens, stack the route rather than shrinking it: title, status,
   action, then supporting detail.

Use Sora for display moments and result headings, DM Sans for product UI and
copy, and DM Mono only for source/provenance, steps, time, status, and system
metadata.

## Source and progress signals

Source rows make trust visible. Show the publisher or source title first; put
the provenance label beneath it in mono; use a quiet verified mark only when it
has a real meaning. Never imply an editorial guarantee merely because a model is
confident.

Progress is directional, not judgmental. Prefer “3 van 5 onderwerpen” and
“klaar om te leren” over score-like language. A linear progress bar needs text
and a current value; colour must not be the only indication of state.

## Dutch voice

Write in short, active Dutch. Lead with **grip**, **kern**, **volgende stap**,
**begrijpen**, **oefenen**, and a concrete learning outcome.

Use:

- “Waar wil je vandaag grip op krijgen?”
- “We maken het onderwerp kleiner.”
- “Deze bron past bij je vraag.”
- “Klaar om te leren.”

Avoid feature lists, AI jargon, claims of certainty without evidence, and
abstract phrasing such as “optimaliseer je leerpotentieel.”

## Inclusive interaction

- Keep text and paired foregrounds at WCAG AA contrast or better in both themes.
- All controls expose a visible focus ring, work with a keyboard, and have a
  useful accessible name.
- Do not make hover the only way to reveal context.
- Honour `prefers-reduced-motion`: animations become immediate and no motion is
  required to understand a state.
- At 320px, actions must remain readable, touch targets stay usable, and wide
  information may scroll horizontally rather than collapse into illegibility.
